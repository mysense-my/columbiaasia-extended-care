What Is Extended Care

What is extended care?

Extended care is hospital-based rehabilitation for people who are medically stable after a stroke, surgery, injury or serious illness but not yet safe to manage at home. Patients stay with us while doctors, therapists and rehabilitation nurses work every day on walking, self-care, swallowing and speech, with a planned return home, usually within weeks.

Think of it as the middle step. The acute hospital treats the illness and keeps the patient safe. Extended care works on what they can actually do again. Home is where they go once they and the family are ready.

Who It's For

Who needs extended care?

Extended care is for anyone who is medically stable but has lost enough strength or function that going straight home would be unsafe. Signs your family member may need rehab before going home: they cannot get to the toilet safely, they cough or choke when eating, they need two people to help them stand, or their speech has been affected. If two or more of these apply, ask the ward doctor about extended care before discharge day.

Your Stay

What happens during an extended care stay?

Every stay starts with an assessment and agreed goals, and every day after that works towards a safe return home. Success is measured by what the patient can do again, not by how long they stay.

1. Assessment on admission

Doctors and therapists check what the patient can and cannot do, and what is realistic.

2. Goals agreed with the family

For example, "walk to the bathroom with a frame" or "eat a meal without help".

3. Daily therapy

Physiotherapy, occupational therapy and speech therapy, planned around the goals.

4. Rehabilitation nursing between sessions

Nurses practise the same goals during daily care, 24 hours a day, so recovery does not stop when the therapist leaves the room.

5. Regular reviews

Rehabilitation physicians and the team review progress and adjust the plan.

6. Caregiver training

Families learn safe transfers, mobility support, daily care techniques and the home exercise plan.

7. Discharge planning

Home set-up, equipment and follow-up are arranged before the patient leaves.

The recovery ladder

Each goal moves up the same four steps, and families see where their loved one is at every review:

Step 1

Takes part in the activity with full support

Step 2

Does it with hands-on assistance

Step 3

Does it alone, under supervision

Step 4

Does it safely and independently, ready for home
